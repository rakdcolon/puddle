The midnight bridge.

Four travelers, one flashlight, a bridge that holds only two. The smallest puzzle with the largest trap.

The instinct most solvers reach for is to use the fastest walker (1 minute) as a permanent courier — send the slow walker across, then ferry the flashlight back, then escort the next slow walker. It feels efficient. It also gives you a total of 19 minutes, and it's wrong.

The trick is realizing that the 10-minute walker is your bottleneck no matter what you do. They'll cost you ten minutes on the bridge whatever happens — so the only question is whether those ten minutes carry one person or two. Pair the two slowest walkers together, and the cost of crossing 10 becomes the same as the cost of crossing 5.

The path

1.
1 and 2 cross. The two fastest go first. They serve as couriers for the flashlight. 1 walks back alone.
+ 3 min
2.
5 and 10 cross together. This is the move that has to be found. The slow pair walks at the slow pair's pace — but they walk once, not twice. 2 walks back alone.
+ 12 min
3.
1 and 2 cross. The couriers reunite on the far side. All four are across.
+ 2 min
Total elapsed 17 min
